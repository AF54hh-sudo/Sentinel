# 2024 Pricing Change Assessment

## Commercial intent, observed behavior, and evaluation design

AMX Tech updated selected packaging and approval guidance in 2024 to make larger deployments easier to close. The change preserved the Basic, Professional, and Enterprise plan structure while giving commercial teams clearer options for seat expansion and term-based negotiation. Management expected the policy to support gross bookings, but it recognized that stronger discount use could cause net revenue to grow more slowly than headline contract value. The assessment therefore separates price, seats, discounts, and plan mix rather than treating every sales increase as equivalent.

Sales leaders reported that Enterprise negotiations used the revised guidance most often. Some exceptions protected strategic renewals; others supported larger initial deployments. Those cases should be evaluated by comparing gross revenue, discount amount, net revenue, customer counts, and average revenue per customer across appropriate periods. A rising discount rate is not automatically harmful if it accompanies durable expansion, but gross activity alone cannot demonstrate improved economics.

The available synthetic business data is observational and covers a limited horizon. There is no randomized control group, and customers may differ across periods. Consequently, the pricing change should not be described as causing a revenue outcome merely because the timing overlaps. Analysts should identify comparison windows, disclose changes in region and plan mix, and avoid allocating shared infrastructure cost to individual plans. Any reported percentage or amount must be calculated from sales records rather than copied from this narrative.

The review recommends continued monitoring of exception frequency, gross-to-net conversion, renewal quality, and downstream support load. It also recommends retaining approval reasons in a future structured field so planned investment can be separated from inconsistent practice. Until then, this document provides policy context and evaluation questions, while the analytical layer supplies the verified financial evidence.
